# T9c: the engine opens a closed container before narration: preregistration (written 2026-10-27, before any build and before any billed call)

Status: written for Brandon's approval of the numbers and gates, then his go on each billed stage (P, S, F below). Nothing below is built. No billed call has been made. It follows the T9b result (`t9b-scope-prereg.md`, plan section "T9b stage F RESULT"): the stage F run failed clause 1 on route A because of one command. This document does not change `t9b-scope-prereg.md`, `t9-enforce-stage1-prereg.md` or their data; their numbers are never pooled with these.

**Decision (Brandon, 2026-10-27):** "`inspect the drawer` should definitely open the drawer also."

## Why

In the T9b full run all 7 unsuccessful turns (6 of 80 on route A, 1 of 20 on route B) were `inspect the drawer`, request kind `open`. In each, the narrator's reply set the drawer to `"state":"open"` in `item_facts` while its prose only said Kristin "decides to open it" or "prepares to open it", so the world became open and the four supplies were never shown. The verifier rejected exactly those turns (0 false rejects of 12). Measured against Brandon's decision, 7 further `inspect the drawer` turns committed with the drawer still closed and nothing shown, so only 1 of the 15 inspect turns did the right thing. The model chooses whether the world opens; nothing in the engine opens it.

The engine already knows two things at the start of the turn: the request kind is `open` (Jev answered `opens_container` true for the container the command names) and whether that container is closed (`world.axis_values(id)["open"] == "closed"`, `world.is_openable(id)`). The engine-performs-prerequisite-steps rule (decision W12; memory `engine-performs-prerequisite-steps`; boarding, standing, taking and seating already work this way in `WorldCapture.before_turn`) says to apply the change deterministically before narration instead of asking the narrator to get it right.

## The change (one change, fixed now)

1. **Engine step.** In `WorldCapture._set_observation_request`, when the kind comes out `open`, the focus is openable and closed, and the protagonist can see it (`world.sees`), the engine calls `world.set_axis(focus, "open")` before the narrator is called. Nothing else moves. No authored sentence is added (the story package has no open text and none is written); the narrator learns of the change because the world now shows the container's contents, so the existing duty line "Show these in your reply: ..." applies (the conditional "If the X opens, show what is inside it" no longer applies because the contents are required, not contingent). The step is a no-op on an open container, on a container that is not openable, on kind `look`, `under`, `enter`, and when Jev answers false or unknown. Story-agnostic: only `is_openable`, the axis and `sees`; no names, verbs, regexes or story vocabulary in engine code.
2. **Rollback.** The engine's whole-turn snapshot (`engine._turn_active`) is taken before `capture.before_turn` and restored on every failure path, so an engine open rolls back like a boarding step. The harness records the container's open state after every turn so this is checked, not assumed.
3. **Jev wording.** The T9b stage F rows show Jev answered `opens_container` true for `inspect the drawer` on 14 of 15 turns while the question's own false text says it "only looks at" the container. That agreement is luck, not design. `opens_container` is reworded so inspecting counts:
   - instructions: `Decide whether the command has the player open the container. Opening it, pulling it out, or inspecting it counts.`
   - true: `The command opens the container, pulls it out, or inspects it.`
   - false: `The command closes, locks, touches, moves, carries, or talks about the container, or goes under, behind, or on top of it.`
   Function name, signature, return type and the `noul` type are unchanged. The wording follows `docs/jev-guard-rules.md` (one sentence stating the job; "yes" means the thing sought). It was written from the decision and from the T9b failure class only; the lists below were written after it and none of their commands appears in an earlier probe's list (the regression set is the one exception, and it is labelled).
4. **Effect on the live game.** `observe_automatically` is True by default, so the engine step runs in the product whether or not `FREYTAG_OBSERVATION_ENFORCE` is set (that switch only turns on the verifier). Nothing here is deployed by this work; production changes need Brandon's explicit instruction, and `FREYTAG_OBSERVATION_ENFORCE` stays unset.

Unchanged and not tested here: the semantic reveal that makes `inspect the drawer` also find the memory card in some turns (engine-authored text rides on the command; the plan notes it); the `under` kind and the `searches_underneath` question (T9b).

**Scope of the rule, stated plainly.** Brandon named `inspect`. The gates below are on `inspect` and the plain opening verbs. `examine`, `search`, `check` and `look inside` are asked and reported, not gated, so the rule can be extended from data rather than from my guess. `look at` and other casual-look verbs are reported too: the wording says nothing about them and the label is not settled.

## Stages S and F: enforced runs with the engine step

**What runs.** As T9b: real engine, product wiring, observation mode on, enforcing verifier on (`live_baseline.py --enforce`), shadow off, local Worker with a hard dollar cap, 70B JSON off, 20 s timeout and no retry on a timeout. New: the engine open step and the reworded question.

**Routes.**
- **A `observe_topup`** (9 commands, unchanged): carries the stage-1 clauses and the first `inspect the drawer` of each replicate.
- **B `observe_under_first`** (4 commands, unchanged): guards the card-find path (`feel under the drawer` must still be classified `under` and not `open`) and has an `inspect the drawer` as its third command.
- **C `observe_inspect_first`** (new, 2 commands): `inspect the drawer`, `feel under the drawer`. Cheap and aimed: it puts 20 `inspect the drawer` turns at the front of a fresh world, then checks the underside search that follows.

**Counts and cost.** Pooled `inspect the drawer` turns: 10 (A) + 5 (B) + 20 (C) = **35**.
- Stage S (billed once, not counted in any rate): A 1 replicate (`--max-calls 90`), B 1 (`--max-calls 40`), C 2 (`--max-calls 40`); local Worker `--max-usd 0.06`; expected about $0.02. Checks plumbing: rows record whether the engine opened the container and the container's open state after each turn; the report builds sheets for the three routes.
- Stage F (billed once): A 10 replicates (`--max-calls 800`), B 5 (`--max-calls 250`), C 20 (`--max-calls 500`); `--max-usd 0.35`; expected about $0.16. No retries, no replacement samples. Stop on any quota code, 429, 5xx or cap and keep the partial result. A turn that fails because enforcement exhausted its recovery is a result, not a stop.
- Output `bench/results/environment-narration/t9c-open/{smoke,full}/{a,b,c}`.

**Procedure.** As T9b: free dry run, billed stage, each a Ringer probe task whose check asserts a stop reason or the exact row count; blind sheets; I read every turn and freeze the labels hash before any verdict is opened; then `join`. Added labels, from narration and command text only: `contents_shown` (every required supply is presented in the committed narration, as `presents_required` already is) and `command_opens` (does the command text tell the player to open the container or inspect it; judged from the command alone).

**Gate (set now).**
- Route A: all stage-1 clauses 1 to 7 unchanged, in particular **clause 1: at most 1 unsuccessful of about 80 observation turns** (the T9b run had 6), clause 2 zero committed narrations missing a required thing, clause 3 tags (zero leak, wrong custody, unrequested move; at most 1 each of refind and invented object), clause 4 rollback, clause 5 at most 2 attempts and 2 verifier requests, clause 6 p95 of no-recovery turns at most 10 s, clause 7 false rejects at most 1 (inconclusive under 5 rejections). S1 and S2 as in T9b.
- **E1, the aim: of the 35 pooled `inspect the drawer` turns, at least 34 end with a committed narration, the drawer open in the committed world, and `contents_shown` true.** Every other turn is listed by id with the reason (unsuccessful; closed after commit; contents not shown). 34 of 35 is about 97%, not a proof of 99%; the plan's 99% target is not provable at this n.
- **E2, engine audit: zero turns where the engine opened a container and `command_opens` is false.** Every engine open must have kind `open` and a recorded closed-to-open change. A violation means the engine changed the world for a command that does not open the container.
- **E3, rollback of the open: for every unsuccessful turn the drawer is closed again and places are unchanged** (extends clause 4; zero violations).
- **S3 (route B), unchanged:** the card is held by the player after the first `feel under the drawer` in at least 4 of 5 replicates with the find shown in prose, zero turns mention the card before it is found. This is the guard that the reworded question did not turn underside searches into `open` turns.
Printed, not gated: share of `inspect the drawer` turns classified `open` and share where the engine opened (a miss here is a Jev miss); first-attempt rejection rate and recovery success rate; turns where the narrator set the container closed after the engine opened it; per-route kind distribution; Jev request count per replicate.

A pass does not deploy anything and does not turn the hosted switch on; it permits writing the T9 confirmation preregistration. A fail on E1 or clause 1 is a stop with the rows and labels recorded.

**Expected, not measured.** E1 close to 35 of 35: the 15 `pull out the drawer` turns in the T9b run (the same kind, with the contents required) all passed on the first attempt. Risks I can see: Jev may answer `opens_container` false for some `inspect` phrasings the list does not cover; the narrator may still omit the supplies once told to show them (the drawer-contents omission rate was 0 of 14, 1 of 20 and 6 of 20 across earlier samples, with the old prompt on `feel under` turns); the narrator may set the drawer closed in `item_facts` after the engine opened it (T9b rows show it writing `"state":"closed"` on 3 of 10 route A inspect turns), which would leave the world closed with nothing required; the semantic reveal can still add the card find to an `inspect`.

**Stated limits.** One story, 70B only, one reader who knows the aim, ten route A replicates share one start (route C 20 and route B 5 share it too). The phrase lists are small and hand-made; a pass shows the wording works on them, not on all player language, and says nothing about pronoun commands ("open it"). Unlocking, keys and locked containers are out of scope. The engine step does not narrate an opening sentence, so a player who inspects a closed drawer reads about its contents without an explicit "she opens it" unless the narrator writes one; whether to add an authored open text to the story package is a separate story-content decision.
